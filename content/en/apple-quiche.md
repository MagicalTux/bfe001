<!-- section: $A991E0 "Apple Quiche" -->

This is my apple pie. We call it the **apple quiche**, because the apples are
baked in an egg and cream *appareil* rather than just sitting on the crust.

It comes in two parts: the **crust** and the **appareil** (the batter poured
over the apples).

<!-- section: $A991E1 "The Crust" -->

The crust can be bought pre-made, which is easier when it's available. How easy
that is depends on the country. In France I used Herta's pre-rolled *pâte
brisée*: it comes on baking paper, so you just unroll it into the pie pan and
you're ready.

If you can't find one, it's simple to make:

- **300 g flour**
- **150 g butter**
- salt
- water

The rule is twice as much flour as butter, by weight. 150 g butter to 300 g
flour is enough for one large pie or two small ones.

1. Rub the butter into the flour until it looks like sand (in French: *sabler
   le beurre*).
2. Dissolve the salt in the water, then add it to the mix bit by bit until the
   dough reaches the right consistency.
3. Roll it out and line the pie pan.

<!-- section: $A991E2 "The Apples" -->

1. Prick the crust all over with a fork.
2. Cut the apples into thin slices, about **2 mm**. A bit thicker or thinner is
   fine, but thick slices may need more time in the oven.
3. Arrange the slices on the crust.

<!-- section: $A991E3 "The Appareil" -->

- **50 g crème fraîche**
- **6 eggs**: 3 whole, plus the yolks only of the other 3
- **60–80 g sugar**, depending on how sweet you want it
- **one** of:
    - amber rum from Martinique (*rhum ambré de Martinique*). Don't use
      Caribbean rum or any other rum; they don't work in this pie.
    - vanilla extract
- *optional:* sliced almonds

Mix everything well, pour it over the apples, and put it in the oven
immediately.

<!-- section: $A991E4 "Baking" -->

- **180 °C for 30 minutes**
- then **150 °C for 30 minutes**

You can let it rest for a while so the eggs set, or eat it right away.

It goes well with vanilla ice cream.
