The week had two threads, plus steady work on the toolchain. On
Sunday, a new GitHub organisation, **LawCodex**, got 61 repositories
at once: legislation as plain Markdown in git, one repository per
jurisdiction. Before that, most of the week went into hardware
research on a Ledger Nano X. The board was mapped and reconstructed
in KiCad (**ledger_nano_x_pads**), a new logic-analyzer driver was
written to decode its buses (**visgrok**), and **reticle**, the
from-scratch HDL toolchain, built FPGA designs that stand in for the
device's MCU. Alongside, **puressh** and **Lode** each finished a
large block of work.

## LawCodex: legislation as Markdown, one repository per country

[**LawCodex**](https://github.com/LawCodex) collects "the laws of
every country as Markdown in git." Each repository is named by its
ISO 3166-1 code (`fr`, `de`, `jp`, …, plus `eu`). It holds the
constitution, codes and laws in force, one file per text with YAML
front matter for the title, official identifier, dates and source
URL, and a `scripts/` directory of re-runnable fetch-and-convert
code. Every README states what is covered, what is missing and why,
and what licence the source texts carry. Sixty-one repositories were
created on Sunday, and together they took about 470 commits, more
than any other project this week.

Two of them show what the work involves:

- [**be**](https://github.com/LawCodex/be) (40 commits) is Belgium
  from the **Justel** database of the Belgian Official Gazette. It
  holds the consolidated texts in force in both French (`fr/`) and
  Dutch (`nl/`), from the Constitution and the codes down to the
  decrees of each region and community. After a first commit with
  the scripts and the Constitution, the import came in as
  half-hourly batches of several hundred files each.
- [**ua**](https://github.com/LawCodex/ua) (12 commits) is Ukraine
  from the Verkhovna Rada's legislation database, retrieved through
  its official open-data API: the Constitution, the codes, then the
  laws in batches. Part of the week was spent on conversion. Text
  quoted inside amending laws no longer turns into headings, and
  deep hierarchies are fitted into Markdown's six heading levels.
  The fetcher identifies itself as LawCodex and never follows
  redirects.

The texts are reproduced faithfully, but they are not an official
publication. The READMEs send readers back to the cited source.

## A Ledger Nano X, pad by pad

[**ledger_nano_x_pads**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/ledger_nano_x_pads)
(new, 57 commits) is "an interactive map of the Ledger Nano X main
board: its 40 test pads, the copper traces between them and the
chips, and a KiCad reconstruction of the whole board," published on
GitHub Pages. It started as a photo flip-viewer with a trace overlay
that could be edited. The log then traces nets one by one: USB,
supply, Bluetooth RF, the OLED's SPI lines, the crystals, SWD and
I²C, the STUSB4500L USB-PD controller and the charger. Once the nets
were traced, the board was rebuilt in KiCad with ground pours,
stitching vias, 3D models and chip markings, behind a three.js
viewer. The README is explicit that this is a reconstruction from
photos, not the manufacturer's design, and that a `?` marks a likely
but unconfirmed name. It also notes that the SWD pads are legacy,
since the STM32 now ships locked. Late in the week the 3D viewer
gained an overlay of two implant specimens, A and B, with their
outlines and solder joints matched to close-ups from Tibane Labs.

[**visgrok**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/visgrok) (new, 47
commits, `v0.1.1`) is the instrument used for this. It is a
from-scratch Rust driver for Sipeed SLogic logic analyzers, built on
[rawusb](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/rawusb), with streaming to
disk, a TUI and a browser UI. It has its own compressed `.vgk`
format and converts to and from sigrok's `.sr`, VCD and raw dumps.
Its decoders were written for the signals this board carries:
auto-baud UART, SPI with D/C and an SSD1306 display decoder, an SD
card bus decoder, ISO 7816-3, and finally Ledger's **SEPROXYHAL**
protocol over ISO 7816, including the authentication handshake. Its
query commands are meant "for people and agents" analysing a capture
from scripts.

## reticle: an FPGA in the MCU's seat

[**reticle**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/reticle) was again the
busiest repository (222 commits, `v0.0.5` and `v0.0.6`), and much of
it pointed the same way. On a Basys 3 board (Xilinx 7-series),
reticle now places, routes and configures CARRY4 chains, RAMB18E1
block RAM with its contents, PLLE2_BASE, DSP48E1 multiplies and
tristate pads. With that, it built a set of IP blocks: an ISO 7816-3
character layer and a terminal that talks to a real card, an
SSD1306-compatible display *slave* that receives what a part draws,
and `seph_mcu`, "the MCU's end of Ledger's SEPROXYHAL link." Put
together in one bitstream behind a 2 Mbaud console, with the
device's buttons pressable from that console, the FPGA "runs the
secure element to its PIN screen" and reads the screen back.

The same week also brought an UltraScale+ fabric model (`uray`)
that writes a bitstream and runs it on a ZCU104, a single-port
USB 2.0 hub and a USB transaction proxy that the kernel's own
drivers bind, SHA-256 and ChaCha20 cores behind a Cynthion's USB
serial port, ECP5 carry chains and DP16KD block RAM, and an inflate
block that will be checked against the compcol corpus.

## puressh: an OpenSSH drop-in

[**puressh**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/puressh), the pure-Rust
SSH suite on purecrypto (65 commits), spent the week closing the
remaining gaps with OpenSSH so it can replace it on a real system.
It now reads any OpenSSH `ssh_config`, including `Include`, `Match`,
tokens, `-o` for every option and connection sharing for sftp/scp.
`sshd` gained OpenSSH's logging (syslog and its `auth.log` lines),
utmp/wtmp/lastlog, every `authorized_keys` option,
`AuthorizedKeysCommand` and `AuthorizedPrincipalsCommand`, and the
`SSH_CLIENT`/`SSH_TTY` environment. `ssh-keygen` handles more key
formats, KRLs and SSHFP records, and scp and sftp are now drop-ins
that run over ssh. There is a `puressh-client` Debian package that
diverts OpenSSH's client programs, Ansible interop runs in CI, and a
`perf:` change removed per-round-trip latency from both client and
server.

## Lode: allocation, destruction and arenas

[**Lode**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/lode), the language
started the week before (41 commits), completed milestone M8, which
covers allocation. Resource types with `deinit` are destroyed at
every scope exit, and use after a move is an error on every path.
Allocation is declared in the signature (`uses alloc
throws(AllocError)`), and a function without `uses alloc` cannot
call one that allocates. `Box`, `List` and `String` come through an
allocator context, and the root heap allocator is itself written in
Lode. Arenas and an out-of-memory policy (`@oom(abort)`) complete
the milestone. Under the abort policy, a `try` on an allocation
compiles to nothing. Refinements, which are facts carried in
signatures and struct fields, now let the standard library skip
re-checking bounds. Underneath, latticefoundry added bulk-memory
operations, SROA, struct returns in registers and a size-aware
inliner.

## Also this week

- **[rustygo](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/rustygo)** — the
  Go-to-Rust compiler got goroutines on worker threads (an M:N
  scheduler with stop-the-world). It shipped at one worker by
  default while races were tracked down. It also gained HTTP/2 over
  TLS, `reflect.MakeFunc`, 127 of Go's own tests passing, and gRPC,
  protobuf, Prometheus and pgx compiling.
- **[catcard](https://github.com/TibaneLabs/catcard)** — a TSS wallet
  can now be the wallet in force on the device. It signs
  transactions and BIP-322 messages together with the other members,
  and session codes travel by SD card or QR (BC-UR or BBQr).
- **[rsasm](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/rsasm)** — x86 APX,
  32-bit Mach-O objects, macOS compact unwind, CodeView line info
  for PE/COFF, and the newest AArch64 families (MOPS, CMPBR).
- **[dnsbox](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/dnsbox)** — TKEY key
  agreement, draft record types, and BIND 9 interop in CI (signing,
  zone tools, `named`, `dig`, `nsupdate`).
- **[mcptk](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/mcptk)** (new) — an MCP
  server toolkit that implements the protocol itself on tokio. It
  supports revision 2026-07-28 alongside the handshake-based
  revisions, over stdio, Streamable HTTP with OAuth, and WebSocket,
  and implements the tasks extension and MCP Apps.
- **[teamclaude](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/teamclaude)** —
  `1.1.24`, with OAuth tokens kept in step across installs.
