A quieter week by volume, about 600 commits, and most of them went
into finishing things rather than starting them. The JavaScript
engine closed most of its remaining test262 gap, with the
internationalization crate under it shipping a release nearly every
day to keep up. The wallet library moved its multi-party wallet
into the browser. On the last day of the week a new project
started: **catcard**, an independent firmware for Coldcard
hardware wallets.

## kataan: from 99.4% to 99.93%

[**kataan**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/kataan), the pure-Rust
JavaScript engine (73 commits), began the week with the README at
99.4% of test262 (51,565 of 51,890). By Sunday the committed figure
was **99.93%: 51,856 passing, 34 known failures**. Each commit
title carries the number of tests it fixed, so the ledger is easy
to follow:

- **Real concurrency where the spec needs it.** `Atomics` now runs
  with real multi-agent execution, and `built-ins/Atomics` is fully
  green. Module evaluation is truly asynchronous now, deferred
  imports evaluate their async dependencies, and the garbage
  collector can collect during execution at bounded safepoints.
- **Intl and Temporal.** CLDR units, calendar fields, hour cycles,
  locale negotiation, `formatRange`, and astronomical
  Chinese/Dangi calendars for Temporal. The Persian calendar moved
  to the 33-year arithmetic rule, and the Islamic leap-day term was
  fixed for pre-epoch years.
- **Regex.** Unicode 17 property tables, emoji string properties,
  and the spec definition of `\s`. A series of performance commits
  also made global match/replace/split linear in the subject length.
- **Quadratic-to-linear fixes.** Repeated string `+=`, per-iteration
  `let` environments, and `Map`/`Set` lookups (now hash-indexed)
  had all been quadratic, and each got its own commit.

The engine tagged `v0.0.8`, and its documentation and a playground
are now published on GitHub Pages.

## intl: seven releases to feed the engine

[**intl**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/intlrs), the pure-Rust,
`no_std` ICU analog (42 commits), went from `0.5.2` to `0.6.1` in
one week, and kataan bumped its dependency several times to pick up
the fixes ("intl 0.6.0 — all nine list combinations, locale
separators (+14)"). The work was ECMA-402 coverage driven by CLDR
48:

- localized **time-zone names** from CLDR metazones, plus the
  zone-name combiner and host DST in a field-level calendar API;
- full ECMA-402 surfaces for **list** and **relative-time**
  formatting;
- **number** formatting: locale NaN/infinity, numbering-system
  symbols, ranges, spec-correct rounding defaults, and the locale's
  `minimumGroupingDigits`;
- **units** in narrow width and compound form, the full ECMA-402
  set;
- **collation**: re-vendored CLDR 48 tailorings, named collations
  via `-u-co-`, and a UTS #10 case level.

It's a clear case of one layer driving another: kataan's Intl
built-ins sit on this crate, so many of the engine's conformance
fixes actually land here.

## libwallet: the MPC wallet moves into the browser

[**libwallet**](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/libwallet), the
multi-chain wallet library with TSS support (37 commits), spent the
week bringing its threshold-signature wallet to the web build. The
steps are numbered in the commits:

- **Stage 1:** a passkey MPC wallet, with a committee of
  [passkey, password, RemoteKey]. The device share comes from a
  WebAuthn PRF passkey.
- **Stage 2:** the wallet persists and unlocks across reloads.
- **Stage 3a/3b:** a multichain wallet that creates the secp256k1 +
  ed25519 key pair and drives the dashboard's Accounts view.

Underneath, distributed FROST keygen and signing now run in the
browser over spotlib's async ceremonies, chain RPC is async through
`rsurl::aio`, and `Account:signAndSendTransaction` is uniform across
EVM, Solana and Bitcoin, on both native and wasm. Ed25519 accounts
got non-hardened HD derivation with tweaked FROST signing,
round-trip verified. On the web side, balances and sends no longer
go through a client-side chain RPC.

## catcard: a clean-room Rust firmware for Coldcard

[**catcard**](https://github.com/TibaneLabs/catcard) started on
Sunday: "an independent, open-source firmware for Coldcard hardware,
written from scratch in Rust", `no_std` on Cortex-M4F, MIT licensed.
The README gives two reasons for it. The first is entropy: it states
that the stock firmware derives its BIP-39 seed from two chained
software PRNGs instead of the chip's hardware TRNG, which leaves
about 22 bits of real entropy on mk3 and about 32 on mk4. That is
why the entropy subsystem is the first thing built. The second is
licensing: the original is MIT plus the Commons Clause, which is
not open source, so CatCard has to be genuinely independent of that
source to be MIT-only.

The first day's scaffold is honest about how far it goes. It has a
build system, the image format, a signing pipeline that produces a
DfuSe image the stock bootloader will accept, and an entropy pool
built from every noise source on the board. There is no wallet yet.
Other commits that day discovered the bootloader's callgate entry
point from its published table (it moves between bootloader
versions, so it is never hardcoded) and mapped the Q1's keyboard,
SE2 and NFC pins.

## OxideAV: embroidery, FBX, and the big video decoders

The media framework accounted for most of the week's volume, spread
across dozens of crates. Three stood out:

- [**oxideav-embroidery**](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-embroidery)
  is new (created 2026-07-30). It handles machine-embroidery design
  formats: Tajima DST, Brother PEC/PES/PHC, Melco EXP with its
  `.inf` companion, and Janome JEF, all decoding to a single typed
  stitch-design model and most encoding back. It also has
  best-effort PHX and a cross-format agreement oracle over
  self-synthesized designs.
- [**oxideav-fbx**](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-fbx) now
  composes the full node transform chain on decode, synthesizes it
  back on encode, and decodes the trailing binary footer. A real
  v7400 fixture now re-encodes byte-for-byte.
- [**oxideav-av1**](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-av1) and
  [**oxideav-h266**](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-h266) kept
  working on their encoders. AV1 gained a multi-tile write arm,
  spatial-scalability layers and segmented loop restoration, with
  the conformance corpus pinned at 122 streams. H.266 added palette
  coding on both sides and multi-slice pictures at 136/136
  externally byte-exact, plus a decode driver for JVET conformance
  streams, all 56 of which now parse their slice headers cleanly.

## Also this week

- **[fullrust](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/fullrust)** builds
  unmodified Rust crates into static, libc-free Linux binaries. It
  ported its toolchain overlay to Rust 1.89, and its container
  image is now built in CI and checked end to end by building a
  proc-macro crate.
- **[zanolib](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/zanolib)** (Zano
  offline transaction signing) was ported in full to Rust,
  byte-for-byte compatible with the Go tree, which was then
  removed. It released as `0.2.0`. Its threshold view key is now
  derived with tsslib's key-image ceremony.
- **[teamclaude](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/teamclaude)**
  `1.1.10`–`1.1.11`: `TC_ACCT` pins a session to one account, even
  in MITM mode, and a burst of 401s no longer rotates the token
  family once per error.
- **[oxideav-h263](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-h263)** added
  RFC 4629 and RFC 2190 RTP packetization alongside Annex K slice
  encoding. **[oxideav-otf](https://github.com/OxideAV/oxideav-otf)**
  added script-itemized shaping and OpenType script, language and
  baseline tag registries.
